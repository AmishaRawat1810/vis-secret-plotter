# Vega Lite Chart Selection Exercises

## 1. Genre Popularity

You are given a dataset of movies with a `Major Genre` field.

Create a visualization that helps answer:

> Which movie genres appear most frequently in the dataset?

---

## 2. Ordered Genre Popularity

Using the same genre data:

> Which genres are the most common, ranked from highest to lowest?

The visualization should make ranking immediately obvious.

---

## 3. Genre Categories with Additional Visual Separation

Using movie genres:

> Create a visualization where genres are visually distinguishable even at a glance.

Use an additional visual encoding beyond position.

---

## 4. Long Category Names

Some genres become difficult to read when labels are crowded.

> Create a visualization that improves readability for category labels.

---

## 5. Movies Released Over Time

Using `Release Date`:

> Show how movie releases change over time.

The chart should emphasize trends.

---

## 6. Comparing Trends Across Genres

Using release dates and genres:

> Show how movie production trends differ between genres over time.

The viewer should be able to compare multiple trends simultaneously.

---

## 7. Volume Over Time

> Create a visualization emphasizing the overall volume of movie releases across time.

Focus more on magnitude than precise comparison.

---

## 8. Genre Contribution Over Time

> Show how different genres contribute to total movie releases over time.

The chart should communicate both:

* total volume
* composition

---

## 9. Budget vs Revenue

Using `Production Budget` and `Worldwide Gross`:

> Explore the relationship between movie budgets and box office revenue.

The chart should help identify:

* correlation
* outliers
* clusters

---

## 10. Budget vs Revenue by Genre

Using the previous visualization:

> Add another dimension that helps distinguish genres.

---

## 11. Budget vs Revenue with Additional Magnitude

Add another quantitative variable such as:

* runtime
* rating

> Show an additional dimension without losing readability.

---

## 12. Multi-dimensional Movie Comparison

Using:

* budget
* revenue
* rating
* genre

> Create a visualization that communicates several variables simultaneously.

---

## 13. Ratings Distribution

Using `IMDB Rating`:

> Show how ratings are distributed across movies.

The viewer should easily identify:

* common ranges
* rare ranges
* skew

---

## 14. Comparing Rating Distributions

> Compare rating distributions between genres.

The chart should allow side-by-side comparison of distributions.

---

## 15. Rating Spread Per Genre

> Show the spread and variability of ratings for each genre.

The viewer should identify:

* median
* range
* outliers

---

## 16. Genre vs MPAA Rating

Using:

* genre
* MPAA rating

> Show relationships between two categorical variables.

The viewer should quickly identify dense combinations.

---

## 17. Quantitative Table Visualization

Using the same genre/MPAA data:

> Create a visualization that behaves like a table but still uses visual encoding strength.

---

## 18. Comparing Subgroups Within Categories

> Compare genre counts split by MPAA rating.

The viewer should compare:

* total counts
* subgroup counts

---

## 19. Part-to-Whole Within Categories

Using the same data:

> Show how MPAA ratings contribute to the total within each genre.

---

## 20. Percentage Composition

Using genre and MPAA rating:

> Compare proportional composition instead of absolute counts.

The viewer should compare percentages, not totals.

---

## 21. Small Multiples: Budget vs Revenue

> Create separate relationship charts for each genre.

The goal is to compare patterns across genres independently.

---

## 22. Small Multiples: Rating Distributions

> Compare rating distributions independently for each genre.

Avoid visual clutter.

---

## 23. Combined Trend Visualization

> Show a trend over time along with an additional summary layer.

Examples:

* average line
* rolling trend
* reference line

---

## 24. Richly Encoded Trend Chart

> Enhance a temporal trend visualization with additional visual cues.

Examples:

* points
* color
* annotations

---

## 25. Genre Proportions

> Show how genres contribute to the whole movie dataset.

Focus on proportional contribution.

---

## 26. Improved Part-to-Whole Visualization

> Create an alternative version of the previous chart with improved readability or aesthetics.

---

## 27. Hierarchical Categories

Imagine genres contain subgenres.

> Show hierarchical proportional structure.

---

## 28. Coordinated Multi-View Display

> Create multiple charts that together tell a richer story than a single chart alone.

Do not use interactions.

---

## 29. Small Dashboard

Create a mini dashboard containing:

* one comparison chart
* one trend chart
* one distribution chart

The charts should collectively summarize the dataset.

---

## 30. Final Polished Visualization

Choose an interesting question about the movie dataset.

Create a polished visualization with:

* thoughtful encodings
* titles
* labels
* formatting
* visual hierarchy

The emphasis should be on communication quality, not complexity.
